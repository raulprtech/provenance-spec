# Why pilot PROVENANCE for scientific articles?

The initial hypothesis is that captured AI assistance and explicit human decisions can be organized into a useful, author-reviewable account with less manual documentation effort. Neither that benefit nor an increase in trust has been demonstrated for PROVENANCE.

## Transparency without judgment for using AI

We want researchers to be able to explain what they delegated, accepted, changed, or rejected without the mere use of AI becoming a basis for punishment. The goal is confidence in an inspectable process, not detecting undisclosed AI use. Scientific methods and claims remain open to scrutiny. The format cannot guarantee institutional protection or override external disclosure requirements.

Trust requires actual practices: agreed capture scope, minimal collection, known recipients and retention, correction of attributions, and author approval before sharing. Missing events are coverage limits, not evidence of misconduct. See the [trust charter](trust-and-transparency.md).

## Example

A model suggests alternative wording for a discussion paragraph. The author edits and applies it. A future integration should record the task, affected paragraph version, permitted change evidence, and that narrow human action. It must not infer that the author verified the science or explain their motives.

The proposed outputs are a detailed working record and a concise, author-approved AI-use disclosure for the article. Neither requires publishing private chats. Coverage is limited to connected tools and declared capture periods.

## What can be tried now?

The existing 0.1.0 CLI builds JSON/Markdown from supplied records. It does not automatically capture article work, run a summarizing model, or enforce author approval. The [article profile](scientific-article-profile.md) describes those future capabilities and their gates. Existing checks have [known gaps](conformance.md); attachments require separate inspection.

The [literature matrix](evidence-base.md) provides motivation and competing prior art, not evidence of this proposal's effectiveness. Fair metadata and artifact packaging alone do not justify another standard.

## Responsible pilot

Use synthetic or explicitly consented, non-confidential article tasks. Compare with manual disclosure and a simple table using the same evidence. Measure captured-event recall, unsupported attributions, correction effort, and privacy failures. Separately evaluate whether participants feel safe sharing, without treating disclosure volume as a trust score. Freeze criteria before confirmatory evaluation.

Exams and ARIA are out of scope. A later educational profile needs a separate study; research documentation cannot establish learning or suitability for disciplinary decisions.
