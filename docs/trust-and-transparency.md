# Trust before detection

PROVENANCE aims to make AI assistance transparent without making the mere use of AI a basis for judging, ranking, or punishing a researcher. We want people to feel able to share what they delegated, which suggestions they accepted or rejected, and which decisions remain their own. Trust is a prerequisite for useful disclosure, not a score computed from a log.

Our design preference is to build confidence in a documented process rather than detect AI use in order to punish it. This is a project commitment and a hypothesis to evaluate, not a demonstrated behavioral effect or a guarantee about how third parties will act.

## What non-punitive transparency means

- Evaluate the scientific claims, methods, evidence, and limitations; do not treat the existence or volume of AI assistance as a quality or integrity verdict.
- Record assistance and delegation without moral labels, suspicious-use scores, authorship percentages, or rankings of researchers.
- Treat missing records as unknown coverage, not proof of hidden assistance or wrongdoing. Declining optional disclosure is not a misconduct signal.
- Distinguish an author's explicit decision from an automated action, an unconfirmed suggestion, and a model's interpretation.
- Let authors correct erroneous attributions and approve the disclosure they share. Shared revisions should identify corrections without retaining confidential removed content.

This does not make scientific claims immune to criticism, excuse fabricated evidence, or override applicable journal or institutional requirements. A record cannot grant immunity from third-party decisions. Before inviting participation, a pilot must state who can access records and whether its actual policies support the promised non-punitive use. If they do not, do not describe that setting as a safe disclosure environment.

## Conditions for a research pilot

1. Explain and obtain agreement to the scope of capture before it starts. Allow pause and exclusion of sensitive work; expose these as coverage limits, not adverse flags.
2. Minimize collection. Do not require full conversations, hidden reasoning, credentials, patient information, confidential prompts, or third-party material.
3. Keep capture separate from sharing. A private working record and an author-approved disclosure are different outputs; automation must not silently publish either.
4. State recipients, storage location, access controls, retention and deletion procedures, and responsibility for responding to accidental disclosures. These controls are requirements for future integrations, not features of the current offline CLI.
5. Offer human correction, accessible review, and a route to contest an attribution. Do not infer motive or understanding from clicks or text changes.
6. Agree that pilot records will not be used to score AI use or punish disclosure. Measure perceived safety and willingness to disclose without coercion; report failures as well as benefits.

The software cannot create institutional trust by itself. Confidentiality agreements, researcher control, and actual practice must support these commitments. Publicly shared material may be copied and cannot be guaranteed retractable.

## Later educational work

Exams and ARIA are outside the initial scientific-article pilot. Any later educational profile needs its own consent, accessibility, policy, and evaluation work. Research documentation results do not establish learning, authorship, or fitness for disciplinary decisions.
