# Literature behind the proposed use cases

Scope update: scientific articles are the initial application. The educational papers below remain background for a deferred ARIA/exam profile, not justification for current educational adoption. Trust-first, non-punitive disclosure is a design commitment; this bibliography does not establish that it increases trust or disclosure, and focused literature review is still needed before making those causal claims.

Localized review, 22 September 2026. These original papers motivate or constrain the use cases; **none evaluates PROVENANCE**. The citations do not establish that this draft improves reproducibility, assessment quality, learning, or academic integrity.

| Paper | What it contributes here | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Wilkinson et al. (2016), [*The FAIR Guiding Principles for scientific data management and stewardship*](https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2016.18), *Scientific Data* 3, 160018. | Principles for making research data and metadata findable, accessible, interoperable, and reusable. Motivates explicit identifiers and machine-readable metadata. | A PROVENANCE record is not automatically FAIR or scientifically reproducible. |
| Soiland-Reyes et al. (2022), [*Packaging research artefacts with RO-Crate*](https://doi.org/10.3233/DS-210053), *Data Science*. | An existing, lightweight approach to linking research artifacts and metadata. Important prior art for any research packaging claim. | PROVENANCE does not yet interoperate with RO-Crate, and its artifact linking is not novel by itself. |
| Perkins, Furze, Roe & MacVaugh (2024), [*The Artificial Intelligence Assessment Scale (AIAS)*](https://doi.org/10.53761/q3azde36), *Journal of University Teaching & Learning Practice* 21(6). | A framework for explicitly setting permitted AI use according to assessment goals. Supports stating a course policy before asking students for a record. | It does not prescribe PROVENANCE or validate process records as evidence of understanding. |
| Furze, Perkins, Roe & MacVaugh (2024), [*The AIAS in action*](https://doi.org/10.14742/ajet.9434), *Australasian Journal of Educational Technology* 40(4), 38–55. | A pilot of assessment policy in one university context. Shows that implementation can be studied rather than assumed. | Results for AIAS in its pilot cannot be transferred to PROVENANCE or to other courses without testing. |
| Bastani et al. (2025), [*Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122), *PNAS* 122(26), e2422633122. | A randomized field experiment that separated AI-assisted practice performance from unassisted exam performance. Motivates keeping documentary evidence separate from a learning claim. | Its mathematics findings do not measure PROVENANCE, all AI tools, or higher-education exams. |
| Liang et al. (2023), [*GPT detectors are biased against non-native English writers*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100779), *Patterns* 4(7), 100779. | An empirical warning about using text-based AI detection in evaluation. Motivates avoiding an authorship or cheating score. | A voluntary provenance record is not itself a validated replacement for misconduct investigation. |

The format's proposed contribution remains narrower than these works: classify the basis of observable statements, link decisions to evidence and limits, and test whether independent readers can reconstruct them with acceptable documentation burden. The [evaluation protocol](evaluation-protocol.md) specifies this still-open test. The [prior-art note](prior-art.md) also covers W3C PROV, RO-Crate, SLSA, and C2PA.
